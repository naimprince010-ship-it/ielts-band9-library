# Video 01 — IELTS Speaking Part 1: Fluency and Confidence

## Production brief

- Lesson ID: `speaking-part1-fluency-confidence`
- Target length: 12–14 minutes
- Format: instructor on camera + clean branded slides + model-answer captions
- Student outcome: begin Part 1 answers calmly, extend them naturally, and avoid filler-heavy pauses
- Important accuracy note: body language and eye contact are not IELTS Speaking scoring criteria

## Recording script

### 00:00–00:35 — Hook

**Instructor:**

“If your mind goes blank as soon as the examiner asks a simple question, this lesson is for you. In the next few minutes, you will learn a repeatable way to begin quickly, develop one clear idea, and sound natural without memorising an answer.”

**On screen:** `Start calmly → Give one idea → Add a reason or example`

### 00:35–01:30 — What the examiner scores

**Instructor:**

“IELTS Speaking is scored using four criteria: fluency and coherence, lexical resource, grammatical range and accuracy, and pronunciation. Eye contact and body language are not scoring criteria. Sit comfortably and communicate naturally, but put your attention on the quality and clarity of your spoken answer.”

**On screen:** Four equal cards showing the four scoring criteria.

### 01:30–03:00 — The short-pause drill

**Instructor:**

“A short pause is normal. During practice, use this response drill: listen to the question, pause briefly, and begin with a direct answer. This is a training technique, not an official three-second IELTS rule.”

**Demonstration question:** “Do you enjoy your hometown?”

**Weak:** “Umm… yes… it is nice.”

**Improved:** “Yes, definitely. What I enjoy most is the strong sense of community.”

### 03:00–05:00 — Natural thinking phrases

Use these only when they fit the question:

- “Well, to be honest…”
- “I’ve never really thought about that, but…”
- “It depends, but generally speaking…”
- “If I had to choose, I’d say…”

**Instructor warning:**

“A thinking phrase should help you enter the answer. It should not become a memorised introduction that you repeat before every response.”

### 05:00–07:30 — The Answer + Reason + Detail structure

**Question:** “Do you like reading?”

**Model answer:**

“Yes, I do, especially in the evening. Reading helps me switch off after work, and I usually spend about half an hour with a novel before I go to sleep.”

**Breakdown:**

1. Answer: “Yes, I do.”
2. Reason: “Reading helps me switch off.”
3. Detail: “I spend about half an hour with a novel.”

### 07:30–09:30 — Guided practice

Pause after each question and give the learner eight seconds to begin:

1. “What do you usually do at weekends?”
2. “Do you prefer mornings or evenings?”
3. “Is your hometown a good place for young people?”

After each pause, display: `Direct answer | One reason | One specific detail`.

### 09:30–11:20 — Common mistakes

- Giving only a yes/no answer
- Using several fillers before reaching the main idea
- Forcing advanced vocabulary that does not fit
- Correcting the same sentence repeatedly
- Reciting a memorised answer that does not address the question

**Instructor:**

“Natural fluency does not mean speaking without any pause. It means keeping your ideas connected and recovering calmly when you need a moment to think.”

### 11:20–12:30 — Final challenge and recap

**Challenge question:** “What is one thing you would like to change about your hometown?”

Ask the learner to record a 20–25 second response using the three-step structure.

**Closing:**

“Begin calmly, give one clear answer, and add a reason or example. Repeat the final challenge three times and compare the recordings. Your goal is clearer organisation and fewer unnecessary fillers—not a memorised performance.”
